Residential Lease Agreement

Riverbend Commons Apartments · 2140 Sagamore Pkwy W · West Lafayette, IN 47906

THIS RESIDENTIAL LEASE AGREEMENT (the "Lease") is made and entered into this 11th day of March, 2024, by and between RIVERBEND COMMONS PROPERTY GROUP, LLC, an Indiana limited liability company ("Landlord"), and the individuals identified in Section 1.2 below (individually and collectively, "Tenant").

Article 2 — Term

2.3 Automatic Renewal. Upon expiration of the Initial Term, this Lease shall automatically renew for a successive term of twelve (12) months upon the same terms and conditions except as to rent, which shall be adjusted to the then-prevailing market rate as determined by Landlord in its sole discretion, unless Tenant delivers written notice of non-renewal to Landlord's management office not less than ninety (90) days prior to the expiration of the then-current term. Notice delivered by electronic mail shall not constitute valid notice hereunder. Tenant's failure to deliver timely notice shall be deemed an affirmative election to renew.

Article 3 — Rent

3.2 Allocation. For administrative convenience only, Landlord may apportion base rent among the individuals comprising Tenant at the rate of Six Hundred Seventy and 00/100 Dollars ($670.00) per bedroom per month. Such apportionment is for accounting purposes only and shall not operate to limit, divide, or otherwise affect the liability set forth in Section 3.4.

3.4 Joint and Several Liability. Each individual comprising Tenant shall be jointly and severally liable for the full and complete performance of each and every obligation of Tenant under this Lease, including without limitation the payment of the entire monthly base rent, all additional rent, all charges, and all damages. Landlord may, at its sole election, proceed against any one or more individuals comprising Tenant for the entire amount owed without first proceeding against, joining, or exhausting remedies against any other individual…

Article 5 — Security Deposit

5.2 Standard Turnover Deductions. Tenant acknowledges and agrees that upon vacating the Premises, the following charges shall be assessed against the Security Deposit in all cases and without regard to the condition of the Premises: (a) professional carpet replacement in each bedroom occupied, at the rate of Two Hundred Twenty-Five and 00/100 Dollars ($225.00) per bedroom; (b) professional cleaning of the Premises at the rate of Three Hundred Fifty and 00/100 Dollars ($350.00); and (c) repainting of all interior walls…

Article 14 — Assignment, Subletting, and Release

14.1 Prohibition. Tenant shall not assign this Lease, sublet the Premises or any portion thereof, or permit occupancy by any person other than an individual named in Section 1.2, whether voluntarily, involuntarily, or by operation of law. Any purported assignment or sublease shall be void and shall constitute a material default. Landlord shall be under no obligation to consider, review, or consent to any proposed assignment, sublease, or substitute occupant, and Landlord's refusal to do so shall not be subject to any standard of reasonableness.

The one thing to know

This lease has no exit. Not one you can use.

Section 14.1 bans subletting, assignment, and substitute occupants, and says outright that the landlord's refusal isn't subject to any standard of reasonableness. Section 14.2 rules out transferring, graduating, or leaving school as grounds for release. Indiana has no statute that gives the right back. If you need out in month four, the lease's answer is: keep paying until July.

What's actually fine

4 provisions
§ 9.1Repair and habitability duties track IC 32-31-8-5 almost word for word. This is the landlord writing down what they already owe you.
§ 7.1The pet ban carves out assistance animals "to the extent required by applicable law." Correct, and not every lease bothers.
§ 14.2The military carve-out cites 50 U.S.C. § 3955 properly. It's federal law they can't waive anyway, but the citation is right.
§ 16.1Venue is Tippecanoe County — where the building is and where you live. Not a forum-shopping clause.

Why this isn't just asking a chatbot to summarize the lease

It reads clauses against each other

§ 3.4 is unremarkable on its own — most group leases are joint and several. § 14.1 is harsh but readable. The finding is the pair: you can be billed for a roommate who left, and you're forbidden from replacing them. A section-by-section summary never puts those two in the same sentence.

It's grounded in one jurisdiction

Every legal note below cites Indiana Code, and half the value is in what the statute doesn't say. Indiana sets no fixed notice period for entry and no cap on late fees — so those clauses aren't illegal, they're just unusually bad. That distinction changes what you ask for.

It ends in a sentence you can send

Understanding a clause doesn't change it. Each finding carries the redline — what to strike, what to put in its place — and a message you can paste into an email before you sign. The product is leverage, not literacy.
